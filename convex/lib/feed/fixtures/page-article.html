The Art of Slow Reading

By A. Writer

Slow reading is a practice, not a speed. When you read slowly, you give each sentence the time it needs to land, and you notice the way an argument is built rather than just where it ends up.

Most of what we read online is built to be skimmed. Headlines promise a takeaway, paragraphs are a sentence long, and the page is arranged so you can leave the moment you have the gist. That is useful for news, but it trains a habit that follows us into everything else we read.

Why it matters

Reading slowly is how ideas become yours. You argue with the author, you reread the paragraph that surprised you, and you put the piece down to think. Bad link and a good link both appear here.

Start small. Pick one long piece a week, read it somewhere quiet, and give yourself permission to take twice as long as you think you need. You will remember more of it, and you will enjoy it more too.

The point is not to read less. It is to read in a way that leaves something behind, so that the next time you sit down with a long piece, you already know how to meet it.
