# Decision log

The single source of truth for this app's decisions. Read the relevant section before designing anything. A decision that changes is **superseded** by a new row that names the old one; rows are never rewritten.

| ID | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | **Dev loop** (2026-11-21): every change goes through the expo-dev-loop plugin's `/expo-dev-loop:feature`: plan against this log, test first, build, review (composition, React Native performance, React Doctor, security), verify on iOS and Android across the screen's state table, and log. The Stop hook enforces the gates and review skills in `.claude/expo-dev-loop.json`. | A repeatable loop, with gates the agent can't skip, makes an agent's "done" checkable. |
| T2 | **Branching** (2026-11-21): `develop` integrates and `main` is what's released. Feature, fix and chore branches come off `develop` and go back by PR (squash-merged). Hotfixes come off `main`, go back by PR and are tagged, then back-merged into `develop`. A release is a PR from `develop` into `main`, merged with a merge commit and tagged. A person merges every PR. The expo-dev-loop branch guard blocks commits and pushes to both branches, and GitHub branch protection holds for everyone. | Released code stays separate from work in progress, and every change to either branch is reviewed by a person. |
| P1 | **Purpose** (2026-11-21): feedSmith is a single-user, minimal RSS reader with a Saved section. It's for reading, nothing else: no highlights, notes, tags, folders or algorithmic ranking. | Feedly drifted toward business intelligence, Readwise Reader toward saving every kind of content, and Pocket became unwieldy. Each feature left out keeps reading the focus. |
| P2 | **Two sections, one gesture apart** (2026-11-21): the app has two main sections, reading Feeds and reading Saved Articles, and moving between them takes one tap or gesture. | Other apps bury read-later behind menus. |
| P3 | **Only Feed Articles can be Saved in v1** (2026-11-21): no share sheet, no pasted URLs. | Keeps v1 to the reading experience; saving from outside the app can come later. |
| P4 | **Reading view is the core** (2026-11-21): Articles open in a Reading view with extracted text and images, with a way to open the original page. | A calm, consistent reading experience is the point of the app. |
| T3 | **Android first** (2026-11-21): v1 targets an Android phone; iOS is not a v1 goal. | The owner reads on Android. |
| T4 | **Convex backend, Effect** (2026-11-21): the backend is Convex, and Effect is used for typed, fallible work. Frontend and backend are all TypeScript. | One language end to end, and Effect models the error-heavy work (fetching and parsing feeds, extracting articles) explicitly. |
| P5 | **Feeds and Saved sections, filtered by chips** (2026-11-21): the bottom bar has two items, Feeds and Saved. Feeds shows All by default, with a horizontal row of chips (each Feed's name and favicon) to show one Feed's Articles. No folders. | Spotify and YouTube-style chips are one tap. The owner follows about a dozen Feeds and checks three closely, so a chip row is enough. |
| P6 | **Adding a Feed** (2026-11-21): you can type a website address (feedSmith finds its feed) or a feed URL directly. No OPML import in v1. | Feed URLs are needed for subscriber-only feeds; OPML can wait. |
| P7 | **Read, not counted** (2026-11-21): opening an Article marks it Read. Read Articles are dimmed, not hidden. There are no unread counts or badges. | Counts turn reading into a chore. |
| P8 | **Done clears Saved** (2026-11-21): marking a Saved Article Done removes it from Saved. No archive in v1; the Article stays in its Feed as Read. | Saved holds only what you still mean to read, so it never becomes Pocket's pile. |
| T5 | **Server fetches and extracts** (2026-11-21): Convex fetches every Feed on a schedule (about every 30 minutes) and builds each Article's Reading view when it's first fetched. | Opening an Article is instant, and the phone never fetches or parses. |
| T6 | **Online-only v1** (2026-11-21): reading needs a connection. Offline Saved reading may come later. | Storing Articles on the phone is extra work on top of Convex. |
| T7 | **Single Google account** (2026-11-21): sign-in with Google, one account, sign-ups closed after it exists. | The Convex backend has a public address; only the owner may use it. |
| P9 | **Pinned Feeds, then newest** (2026-11-21): up to 3 Feeds can be Pinned; their chips follow All in the order you set. Other chips sort by their newest Article. Pin and unpin in Manage Feeds or by long-pressing a chip. | The owner checks about three Feeds closely; the rest can follow activity. |
| P10 | **Managing Feeds** (2026-11-21): a "+" chip at the end of the row adds a Feed. A Manage Feeds screen, reached from an icon in the Feeds top bar, renames, removes, pins and orders them. | Adding is one tap; managing stays out of the way of reading. |
| P11 | **Save and Done by swipe or button** (2026-11-21): swipe an Article in the list to Save it, or use the Save button in its Reading view. Done works the same way in Saved. | Triage without opening; a button when you're mid-Article. |
| P12 | **An Article in the list** (2026-11-21): lead image (when there is one), title, Subtitle (two lines at most, left out when the Feed has none), the Feed's favicon and name, and when it was published. | The imagery the owner wants, without a wall of text. |
| P13 | **Reading view contents** (2026-11-21): title, Subtitle, Feed, publish date, lead image, body text and images, Save or Done, and a way to open the original page. Nothing else. Light or dark follows the phone. | Reading is the whole job of the screen. |
| P14 | **Keeping Articles** (2026-11-21): Articles that aren't Saved are deleted 30 days after publication. Saved Articles stay until marked Done. Saved shows the most recently Saved first. | All stays quick to scroll; nothing you chose to keep is lost. |
| P15 | **Refresh and broken Feeds** (2026-11-21): pulling down on Feeds fetches every Feed now. A Feed that stops working shows a quiet warning in Manage Feeds only and keeps its existing Articles. | You never wait on the schedule, and failures never clutter reading. |
| T8 | **Reading view source** (2026-11-21): use a Feed's full text when it has it; extract from the web page only when the Feed carries just a Subtitle or summary. | Subscriber feeds (for example The Verge's full-text feed) carry the whole Article, and their pages sit behind paywalls. |
| T9 | **Feed URLs are secrets** (2026-11-21): every feed URL is stored only on the server, never logged and never shown in full. v1 supports URL-only access (no username and password). | Subscriber feeds often put a personal key in the URL. The Verge's doesn't, but others do. |
| D1 | **Design references** (2026-11-21): Todoist, Superhuman Mail (its recent Android update) and YouTube (its recent updates) are the bar for Android UI quality. Follow current Material 3 Expressive principles, but the app must not look generically Google. | The owner wants a great Android app with its own identity, not a stock Material template. |
| D2 | **Brand palette** (2026-11-21): Midnight `#1D2532`, Navy `#333D5F`, Forest `#2D423E`, Sage `#50716C`, Gold `#CBB07B`, with a warm cream for light surfaces. Sampled from the owner's reference image (navy, forest green and gold table setting). | Calm, deep and warm: suited to focused reading, and unlike default Material colours. |
| D3 | **Dark first, both themes** (2026-11-21): feedSmith follows the phone's light or dark setting. Dark (Midnight and Navy surfaces, cream text) is designed first; light uses cream surfaces with Midnight text. | The palette is at its best dark, and much reading happens in the evening. |
| D4 | **Brand palette in v1; themes through Uniwind** (2026-11-21): v1 uses only the brand palette, never wallpaper (Material You) colours. Styling uses Uniwind, whose themes leave room for wallpaper colours or other themes later. | Wallpaper colours aren't critical for v1, and Uniwind keeps the option open without rework. |
| D5 | **Gold is the one accent** (2026-11-21): Gold marks only what's active or yours: the selected chip, the Saved state, the active bottom bar item. Navy, Forest and Sage carry surfaces and secondary details. | Used rarely, the accent always means something. |
| D6 | **Reading comfort decides type** (2026-11-21): the Reading view's typeface, size, measure and spacing are chosen for ease and delight of reading; the interface avoids Roboto. No particular font is mandated. | Reading is the product. |
| D7 | **Motion on action, stillness while reading** (2026-11-21): Material 3 Expressive motion appears on interactions (chips, Save and Done swipes, the bottom bar, opening an Article). The Reading view itself doesn't move. Decided case by case with the design-with-taste skill. | Delight when you act; nothing competes with the text. |
| D8 | **Native behaviour, own identity** (2026-11-21): keep what makes an Android app feel native (predictive back, edge-to-edge, the bottom bar, haptics, system gestures). Drop the stock look (wallpaper colours, Roboto, pastel tinted containers everywhere). Standard components like a FAB are fine when they're the best UX. Todoist is the model of this balance. | A great Android app with its own brand, not a Material template. |
| D9 | **8-point grid, spaced by relationship** (2026-11-21): every space is a step of one scale on an 8-point grid, named by relationship: `inside` 4 (parts of one element, the only 4-point step), `related` 8 (items in one group), `group` 16 (separate groups), `section` 32 (major sections), and `edge` 16 (the screen's side margins). The closer two things are related, the closer they sit, and the space between two things is never smaller than the space inside either of them. The scale lives in `src/theme/spacing.ts`, mirrored in `src/global.css`; Tailwind's numeric steps still compile (Uniwind needs the base `--spacing`), so a test rejects them (Q24). | Proximity shows structure without lines or boxes (Gestalt), and one coarse scale keeps every screen in the same rhythm. Naming steps by relationship makes code say why two things are a given distance apart. |
| D10 | **Google's button by Google's rules** (2026-11-21): the "Sign in with Google" button follows Google's branding guidelines exactly: Google's colours for each theme, the full-colour G, Roboto Medium (Android's `sans-serif-medium`) at 14/20, a 40dp pill, 12dp side padding and 10dp between logo and label. It's the one element exempt from D2–D6 (palette, type), D9 (spacing) and Q19/Q24; its spec lives alone in `src/theme/google.ts`. | Google requires its button to look like Google's, and people trust the button they recognise. Keeping the exception in one file stops it spreading. |
| T10 | **Crash reporting with Sentry** (2026-11-21): crashes go to Sentry (`@sentry/react-native`, free plan, source maps uploaded during EAS builds). Reports carry only the stack trace, app version, device, Android version and screen; no feed URLs, Article text, identity, IP, Session Replay or screenshots. The owner creates the Sentry account. | Mature and well supported. EAS Observe was the alternative; it may come later for launch metrics only. |
| T11 | **Phone, portrait, Pixel 10 as the reference** (2026-11-21): v1 is phone-only and portrait-only on the Android versions Expo SDK 58 supports. Budgets are measured on the owner's Pixel 10 (Android 17). | One shape, done well, on the device that matters. |

| T14 | **Convex Auth v2 with Google** (2026-11-21): sign-in uses Convex Auth v2 (`@convex-dev/auth`), the current stable release, with its Google OAuth method through the dev deployment's callback (`https://<deployment>.convex.site/oauth/google/callback`) and one Google Web application client. Refines T7. | It's Convex's own auth, now stable, with no signing keys to manage. Google keeps T7's one-tap sign-in. | P16 | **Personal, built for many users** (2026-11-21): feedSmith is a personal reader: each user's Feeds, Saved Articles and Read state are their own, with no shared, social or algorithmic features. It's built so more than one person can use it, though only the owner does today. Supersedes P1's "single-user"; the rest of P1 (reading only: no highlights, notes, tags, folders or ranking) stands. | The owner wants the backend ready for other people without a rewrite. Keeping each user's reading private keeps the product personal. |
| T15 | **Multi-user backend, sign-ups by allowlist** (2026-11-21): every Feed, Article, Saved and Read row belongs to a user, and every Convex function reads and writes only the signed-in user's rows. No code knows who the owner is. Who may sign up is one setting: an allowlist of Google emails in a Convex environment variable, checked in Convex Auth's `createUser` (today: the owner only; later others, or anyone). Sharing work across users (fetching a Feed once for everyone who follows it) waits until others use the app. Supersedes T7's "one account, sign-ups closed after it exists" and T14's "the single account is enforced in `createUser`"; Google sign-in (T7, T14) stands. | Opening feedSmith to others becomes a setting, not a migration. The allowlist still keeps strangers off a public backend whose scheduled fetching costs money (T5). |

| P17 | **The Account sheet** (2026-11-21): a circle at the top right of the Feeds and Saved top bars shows the User's initial (Gold on Navy in dark, deep gold on sand in light; D5's "yours"), labelled "Account, {name}" for TalkBack. It opens Android's bottom sheet with the User's name and email, **Sign out** and **Delete account**. No profile photo is stored or loaded. | Where Android users look for their account, without a third bottom-bar item (P5). A sheet suits one-off actions and keeps the User where they were. The initial needs no extra data or network fetch. |
| P18 | **Deleting an Account** (2026-11-21): **Delete account** asks once, with a native confirmation ("Delete your account and everything in it? This can't be undone."), then immediately and permanently removes the User, their sessions and Google sign-in link, and all their Feeds, Articles, Saved and Read state, and signs them out. There's no grace period. An email still on the allowlist can sign up again from scratch. | A personal reader holds nothing worth keeping once its User wants it gone, and a real delete is the honest privacy promise (P16). |
| Q-bar5 | **Quality bar after FEE-21** (2026-11-21): Q25 treats deep-link parameters as untrusted, Q26 keeps session tokens in SecureStore with expiring sessions (a release blocker), and Q27 names third-party brand elements (D10) as the only exceptions to Q19 and Q24, and Q28 checks that deleting an Account leaves nothing of it (a release blocker). The bar now holds Q1–Q28. | Gaps FEE-21's quality-bar review found. |
| T16 | **Sentry with PII, logs and Session Replay** (2026-11-21): crash reports go to Sentry (`@sentry/react-native`, source maps uploaded during EAS builds) with `sendDefaultPii` on (IP address, user and device details), Sentry Logs on for `Sentry.logger` calls only (console output isn't forwarded, so a library can't leak a session token, Q26), and Session Replay on (10% of sessions, every session with an error) under Sentry's default masking of all text, images and inputs. There's no scrubber. The feedback widget and screenshots stay off. Deleting an Account (P18) doesn't reach Sentry: its events expire with Sentry's retention, or the owner deletes them by hand on request. Supersedes T10's "only the stack trace, app version, device, Android version and screen" and its ban on IP, identity and Session Replay; Sentry as the crash reporter (T10) stands. | The owner wants the fuller debugging context while feedSmith has one user. Replay's default masking keeps what's read off the recording. |
| Q-bar6 | **Quality bar after T16** (2026-11-21): Q12 now asks for readable crash reports and masked replays instead of a minimal, scrubbed report; Q9 drops "never sent in crash reports"; Q28 names Sentry's data as its one exception. The bar still holds Q1–Q28. | T16 overrules T10. |
| T17 | **Article content as structured blocks** (2026-11-21): the server turns a Feed's HTML into a small set of blocks (paragraph, heading, list, quote, image, and bold, italic and link spans) and stores only those; the app draws them with its own components, never a WebView or HTML renderer. Anything outside the set (video, table, embed, code, anything else) becomes an `omitted` block in its place, which the Reading view shows as a quiet line with **Open original**, and each Article records the kinds it omitted. | The allowlist is the block format itself, so a page's markup can't reach the app (Q8), and the Reading view keeps the app's type, colour and spacing. The one native HTML renderer (`react-native-render-html`) hasn't been released since January 2022. Recording what's omitted shows which block types are worth adding. |
| T18 | **Fetch policy** (2026-11-21): every outbound fetch goes through one Effect HTTP service. It accepts only `https:` URLs on the default port, with no username or password, whose host is a domain name (never an IP address, `localhost`, or a `.local`, `.localhost` or `.internal` name); follows at most 3 redirects, re-checking each; gives up after 10 seconds; and stops reading at 2 MB. A host name that resolves to a private address (a wildcard DNS name such as `127.0.0.1.nip.io`) isn't caught, since Convex actions can't resolve DNS first; Convex's hosted fetch limits what such a request could reach. A fetch keeps a Feed's newest 100 Articles, each guid once, within a stored-size budget. Adding a Feed fetches it first, so a URL that isn't a working feed never becomes a Feed. | User-supplied URLs are fetched from the server (SSRF), and Feed content is untrusted (Q8). The caps bound what one Feed can cost. |
| T19 | **Each Feed on its own pace** (2026-11-21): each Feed is checked about twice per expected new Article, judged from its Articles in the last 14 days, over the span they cover when that's shorter (many feeds list only their newest Articles; at least a day, so one fresh Article isn't a burst), never more often than every 15 minutes and never less than every 12 hours. Each Feed keeps its own next check time; a cron every few minutes fetches the Feeds that are due. A failing Feed's next check backs off (Q7), and it returns to its pace once it works again. Checks send the `ETag` and `Last-Modified` the Feed last gave, so an unchanged Feed answers "304 Not Modified" with no body. Pulling down on Feeds still fetches every Feed now, four at a time, skipping any fetched in the last two minutes so repeated pulls can't hammer a site. Supersedes T5's "on a schedule (about every 30 minutes)"; the rest of T5 (the server fetches and builds the Reading view, never the phone) stands. | A news site and a monthly blog shouldn't be checked alike: busy Feeds stay fresh within minutes, quiet ones aren't fetched for nothing, and conditional requests make frequent checks nearly free for feedSmith and for the sites. |
| T20 | **Long lists use Legend List** (2026-11-21): All and every later long list render with Legend List (`@legendapp/list`, pure JavaScript, no native module). | The owner's choice for the list provider; it virtualises without a native rebuild, and its scroll budget is measured on the Pixel 10 (300 Articles, no dropped frames). |
| D11 | **An Article row in All** (2026-11-21): a line with the Feed's favicon (its initial when it has none), name and how long ago it was published (the phone's own words: "2 hr. ago"); the title in Literata; the Subtitle in two lines at most; the lead image as a 72dp rounded thumbnail at the right. Read rows dim their title to the muted text colour and fade their image; they're never hidden and nothing counts them (P7). Favicons load from the Feed's own site (`/favicon.ico`), never a third-party favicon service (Q13). Refines P12's layout. | The owner chose thumbnails beside the text over large images: four or five Articles a screen, and rows with and without images line up. Dimming by colour keeps Read titles at a tested contrast (Q14). |
